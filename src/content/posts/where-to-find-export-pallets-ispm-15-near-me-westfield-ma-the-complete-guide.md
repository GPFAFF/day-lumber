## What ISPM 15 Means and Why Compliance Is Non-Negotiable

ISPM 15 — the International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 — is a globally enforced regulation developed by the International Plant Protection Convention (IPPC). It requires all wood packaging materials used in international trade, including pallets, skids, and crates, to be treated and officially certified to eliminate the risk of transporting invasive insects and plant diseases across national borders.

Businesses exporting goods on wooden pallets to the European Union, Canada, Australia, China, Japan, and dozens of other countries must use ISPM 15 compliant pallets. Non-compliant wood packaging is seized or destroyed at the port of entry — along with every product loaded on it.

Key compliance facts every Westfield exporter must know:

- **Heat treatment (HT)** requires wood to reach a core temperature of 56°C sustained for 30 continuous minutes, eliminating all regulated pests
- **Methyl bromide fumigation (MB)** is an approved alternative but is being actively phased out across major markets due to environmental and health concerns
- **The official IPPC mark** must be physically stamped on every certified pallet, displaying the country code, accredited producer number, and treatment method used
- **Only accredited producers** can legally stamp and certify pallets — self-certification is not permitted under any ISPM 15 guidelines
- Compliance applies to all wood packaging including pallets, skids, crates, dunnage, and wood blocking used in international shipments
